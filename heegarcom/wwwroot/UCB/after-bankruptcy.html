The traps

Chains dressed up as fresh starts.

After discharge, the offers come fast. Some are chains with a bow on top — learn to spot them on sight.

!

High-fee credit cards

"Guaranteed approval" cards loaded with setup and monthly fees eat your rebuild before it starts.

!

Subprime auto loans

Sky-high interest on a car you're told you're "lucky to get." The lender wins; you tread water.

!

High-APR store cards

The checkout discount costs far more in interest than it ever saves you.

!

Fee-and-penalty loans

Loans built on late fees and penalties are designed for you to slip and pay more.

The scams

If it sounds too good, it's a leash.

No one can legally erase an accurate bankruptcy. Anyone who says otherwise is selling you back into debt.

×

"We'll erase your bankruptcy"

An accurate discharge stays on your report for years. This promise is always false.

×

Upfront-fee "credit repair"

Charging before any work is done is a classic red flag — and often against the law.

×

"Limited-time" pressure

Urgency is a sales tactic. A real path to freedom will still be there tomorrow.

×

Too-good-to-be-true promises

Guaranteed scores, instant fixes, "secret" tricks — all bait for the same trap.

The don'ts

Four quiet moves that set you back.

Small habits, big consequences. These are the ones that trip people up most.

×

Don't cancel old cards

Older accounts lengthen your credit history — closing them can drop your score.

×

Don't max them out

Keep balances low against your limits. High utilization drags your score down.

×

Don't pay late

Payment history is the single biggest factor. On-time, every time, rebuilds fastest.

×

Don't open cards in a rush

Several new accounts in a short window looks risky and dents your progress.

Straight answers

After-bankruptcy questions, answered.

Can anyone remove a bankruptcy from my credit report?

No. An accurate bankruptcy stays on your report for years; anyone promising to erase it is running a scam.

How soon can I rebuild credit after discharge?

Right away — with the right habits and the right accounts. The first months matter most.

What's the fastest way to fall back into debt?

High-fee "fresh start" offers, emotional spending, and missed payments. Avoid those three and you're most of the way there.
